01 A note before
the numbers

A little while ago one of your LinkedIn posts came across my feed. I wasn’t looking for work — I just kept reading. The thinking was clear, generous and unusually honest about how women actually build businesses. So I did the thing good writing makes you do: I clicked through to your profile to find out more.

That led me to your website. You already have a home on the web — a real one, in your own voice — so this was never about filling a gap. It was more presumptuous than that: I kept imagining how the site might feel if it moved the way your coaching does. So instead of sending a pitch, I built the alternative first — a new twist on what you already have — and only then let myself write to you.

The more I read, the more this new version wanted a specific shape. Your work moves people from competing advice and noise, through the signal of what is actually true for them, into a decision, and then into sustainable movement. That isn’t a template. So I designed around it — a Red Thread framework, with Align → Strategise → Act as a proposed organising spine — and made the site behave like the coaching it represents.

I built the reimagining first, then earned the right to send the invoice second. You are holding the invoice — the new version is already yours to see.
On why this document exists at all

So this statement has one job: to make the price make sense. Custom work like this usually arrives as a single intimidating number with no explanation. I’d rather show you the seams — every part of the work, what a studio would ordinarily charge for it, and then the deliberately small figure I’m asking, because this began as a gesture, not a sale. No pressure sits behind any of it. If it isn’t right for you, that is a completely fine answer.

The pages that follow: 02 — the creative idea I am proposing. 03 — what was actually built, in plain terms. 04 — the cost, split line by line, with the reason for each. 05 — friendly terms, and how little is due to begin.

02 The creative idea
I am proposing

The Red Thread is a proposal, not an assumption.

As I read through your writing, I noticed a consistent movement beneath the individual subjects you discuss. Women often arrive surrounded by noise — competing advice, external expectations, too many possible directions and the pressure to make the “right” move. Your work appears to help them separate that noise from what is genuinely true — for the person and for the business — so they can make a clearer decision and turn it into sustainable action.

Working title The Red Thread Decision Studio

The “red thread” is not something I am claiming as your existing methodology, brand name or intellectual property. It is my creative interpretation of the connective quality I see in your work — the line that brings personal alignment, commercial strategy and focused action into one coherent direction.

The red comes from the visual identity already associated with your brand. The thread became the metaphor for continuity — one idea travelling through the entire experience, rather than a set of separate pages.

Why I believe the concept fits

  • Makes an intangible service tangible — visitors grasp the movement before the individual offerings.
  • Connects the person and the business — inner alignment without losing commercial seriousness.
  • Gives one memorable organising idea — every page, interaction and call to action belongs to it.
  • Sets you apart from standard coaching sites — a point of view, not service cards and carousels.
  • Grows with the business — new services, articles and tools slot in without weakening the story.

This is the creative direction I am putting forward — not a decision I have made on your behalf. If the language feels true, we can refine it together and make it yours. If it does not, the underlying website and strategic architecture remain valuable; we can rename or reinterpret the metaphor without starting again. The Red Thread is my opening proposition — the beginning of a conversation, not the conclusion of one.
